# Your Mod, Your Work — Mossy's Ownership & Credit Policy

## Everything You Create Is Yours

When you build a mod with Mossy's help — whether she wrote a Papyrus script with you, walked you through setting up weapon records, helped you design a quest, generated texture advice, or guided you through the entire pipeline — **the finished work belongs to you, completely and without exception.**

Mossy is a tutor and assistant. You are the creator. The ideas, the vision, the creative decisions, and the final product are yours. You have full rights to:

- **Publish** your mod anywhere — Nexus Mods, Bethesda Creations, any platform
- **Sell** it through the Bethesda Verified Creator Program or any other legitimate platform
- **License** it however you choose (open permissions, no permissions, anything in between)
- **Modify** it, expand it, rebuild it from scratch
- **Collaborate** on it with others under whatever terms you set
- **Keep it private** — you never have to release anything you make

No one from Mossy will ever claim ownership, demand royalties, or restrict what you do with your work.

## The One Small Thing We Ask

If Mossy helped you build something you're proud of — and you feel like giving a nod — we'd love it if you dropped a brief mention in your mod's description or credits section.

Something like:

> *"Created with assistance from Mossy, the Fallout 4 AI modding tutor."*

Or shorter:

> *"AI guidance: Mossy"*

Or just a line:

> *"Thanks to Mossy for the scripting help."*

That's it. That's genuinely all we ask — a line of text if you feel it's deserved. It helps other modders discover Mossy, and it means a lot to see what people built with her help.

**This credit is entirely optional.** If you'd rather not include it, that's completely fine. Your mod is yours regardless.

## Why This Policy Exists

A lot of AI tools are vague about who owns the output. Mossy isn't vague: **you own it.**

Mossy's purpose is to help you become a better modder and to help the Fallout 4 community grow. That goal is best served by empowering creators, not by attaching strings to their work.

The modding community runs on generosity — people sharing knowledge, tools, fixes, and assets freely. Mossy is part of that tradition. She gives you her knowledge. You take it, use it, build something amazing, and share it with the world.

## Third-Party Assets and Tools

Mossy's policy covers the work *she helped you create*. It does not cover:

- **Bethesda's assets** — vanilla game content is Bethesda's property. Modding permissions are governed by Bethesda's modding terms. Mods may only be distributed on permitted platforms.
- **Community tools** (xEdit, BodySlide, F4SE, Community Shaders, etc.) — these have their own licenses; credit their authors appropriately.
- **Community assets** you used (textures, meshes from other mods) — always check each asset's license and credit accordingly.
- **Voice acting** you recorded using other people's voices or AI models of existing actors — follow the tool's license terms.

When in doubt about a third-party asset's license, check the original mod page on Nexus or the tool's GitHub README.

## A Note on AI-Assisted Content and Nexus / Bethesda Policies

As of 2026, Nexus Mods and Bethesda's Verified Creator Program both allow AI-assisted content creation as long as:
- You own or have rights to all underlying assets
- The finished mod complies with platform content guidelines
- You are the publishing author

If either platform updates their policies, always check their current terms before publishing.

## Share What You Make — The Community Grows With You

The Fallout 4 modding community runs on generosity. Every guide written, every asset shared, every bug fix posted, every "here's how I solved it" comment in a forum thread makes the next person's journey easier. If Mossy helped you figure something out, pay it forward.

**Ways to give back:**

- **Publish your mod** — even a small weapon retexture or a one-room dungeon is something someone out there has been searching for.
- **Write a description or readme** — explain what your mod does, how you made it, and what you learned. Future modders will thank you.
- **Answer questions** — on Nexus Mods comments, Reddit (r/Fallout4Mods), or Discord. You know more than you think you do.
- **Share your workflow** — if you discovered a faster way to bake textures, generate LOD, or structure a quest, post about it. Your tip might save someone three hours.
- **Credit the tools and guides that helped you** — ElminsterAU, kinggath, doodlum, every author whose work you stood on. They gave their time freely; a line of credit costs nothing and means everything.

The best mods inspire more mods. The Fallout 4 community is still going strong over a decade after the game launched — and it's because people keep sharing.
